Standing Power Throw Technique and Practice

The standing power throw asks you to launch a ten pound medicine ball backward and over your head as far as you can. It looks simple, but the distance comes from the legs and hips far more than from the arms. This page covers setup, technique, common errors and a short practice routine.

Setup

Stand with your back to the throwing area, feet about shoulder width apart, holding the ball in both hands at hip height. Keep your toes behind the line. Your chest should be tall, your knees soft, and your eyes forward until the release. You get several attempts, and your best throw usually counts, so use the first as a controlled test.

The movement in three beats

First, sink into a quarter squat while the ball drops between your knees. Second, drive up through the floor with a fast extension of the hips and knees, and let the arms follow the legs. Third, swing the ball up and back overhead and release at about forty five degrees, finishing on your toes. A smooth chain from legs to hips to arms matters more than a dramatic arm swing.

Common errors

Throwing too flat sends the ball into the ground early. Throwing too high wastes energy straight up. Stepping over the line voids the attempt. Pulling with the arms alone gives a short throw and sore shoulders. Many people also forget to breathe, which tightens the trunk at the wrong moment, so exhale sharply as you release.

Practice routine

Train twice a week. Begin with ten light jumps and ten hip hinge swings with no ball. Then do six throws at eighty percent effort, focusing on the leg drive. Add four all out throws and have a friend mark distances with chalk or tape. Finish with kettlebell swings or Romanian deadlifts for three sets of eight, since posterior chain strength carries straight into distance. Rest at least forty eight hours before the next session.

Using your numbers

After a practice session, note your best throw in meters and compare it with the scale for your age band. Small gains of twenty or thirty centimeters can raise your score noticeably. To see the points your distance earns, you can look up the scoring tables.
